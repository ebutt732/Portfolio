01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

One system, three very different jobs

Sentinel runs security operations for a franchised company. Three roles, three very different jobs:

  • ✴️Franchise Owners (Supervisors): manage sites and shift assignments in a franchise
  • 👮🏻Dedicated Officers: hold a fixed post and scan checkpoints all shift
  • 🚗Patrol Officers: move between sites on a scheduled runsheet

My job was to design a single coherent experience for all three, across web, mobile, and tablet.

02 / PROBLEM · CHALLENGE · GOAL

Fragmented visibility, memory-based shifts

  • Fragmented dashboards – owners stitched together the ground truth from disconnected views.
  • Memory as an interface – officers had to remember steps instead of following them.
  • Failure discovered late – missed checkpoints surfaced after the fact, too late to fix.
Goal 01

Real-time visibility

Owners should know what's happening the moment it happens – not the next morning.

Goal 02

Effortless shifts

Officers should complete a shift with everything laid out, step by step. No guessing.

The system technically worked – but cognitively, it was exhausting.
03 / PROCESS · EXECUTION

When stakeholders disagree, design the argument

Redesigning patrol runsheets, the client wanted zoned routes with one fixed start point. We knew it would increase travel time and break the route optimizer. Instead of debating it, we designed both options and let the trade-offs speak for themselves.

Client's ask – Option A

Zoned runsheets, single start point. Simpler on paper; longer patrols, broken route optimization in practice.

Our counter – Option B · adopted

Dynamic starting points per officer, derived from the assigned runsheet. Optimized routes, less travel.

Outcome

Seeing the same sites with two different routes made the trade-off visible. The client aligned – no argument needed.
